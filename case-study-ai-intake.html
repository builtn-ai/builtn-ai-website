AI operations case study

From blind receiving to 30-second AI identification.

How an overnight warehouse test became the largest operational cost savings in company history — conceived, tested and deployed in under two weeks.

Prior operating experience led by Built-N-AI founder Casey Jackson. Company identity is omitted.

The challenge

Receiving was a costly black box.

For a company buying truckloads of mixed consumer electronics — mostly open-box and customer-return product — each truck arrived with thousands of units and no manifest worth trusting. Until the product was physically in hand, there was no reliable way to know exactly what had been purchased.

Identifying each item required multiple human touches: reading packaging, cross-referencing part numbers, researching manufacturer data and hand-keying information into the ERP. With 2,000 units on a truck, the process took 3–4 weeks from receipt to sale-ready state, tying up labor, slowing cash flow and creating downstream data-quality problems.

Incomplete or inaccurate product records also meant thinner marketplace listings, affecting impressions, conversion and sell-through.

“We had no idea what we were receiving until it was physically in our hands. The labor cost was enormous and the data quality downstream was hurting our sell-through.”

The insight

A late-night warehouse test.

Five electronics items were pulled from inventory. Using only a smartphone and an AI platform, photographs of the retail box and physical item were submitted directly to AI.

The response came back in under 30 seconds — comprehensive enough to test whether AI could eliminate much of the identification and data-entry work.

Data returned in 15–30 seconds

  • Full manufacturer part number
  • Manufacturer name
  • Full product description and specifications
  • A listing title ready for online marketplaces
  • UPC codes
  • MSRP
  • Used / resale market price
  • A confidence rate on the identification itself
100% accuracy in the initial five-item test. No barcode scanner. No prompt refinement. Photographs alone were sufficient for full product identification in the test.

From test to production

Under two weeks from idea to live workflow.

After the proof of concept, a full requirements document was written and handed to the company’s lead developer. His initial expectation was that image-based identification would top out around 25% accuracy — too low for production use.

In under one week, a new Advanced Intake module was built and integrated directly into the company’s Dolibarr ERP platform. On first launch, the system performed at more than 90% accuracy.

“Getting my lead developer excited and onboard was as important as the cost savings. Once he saw what this technology could deliver, the whole organization shifted.”

What the tool does

Photograph the product once. Nobody keys it in again.

The module was not an identification lookup bolted onto an existing process. It replaced the receiving process. A worker labels a unit, photographs it, and puts it down — the product record and the purchase order are built from that, and the review that used to mean re-handling the item now happens on a screen.

01

Label and photograph

A unique barcode goes on the unit, then photos of the retail box and the product itself are captured on a phone. This is the only time a person handles the item.

02

AI identifies it

In 15–30 seconds: manufacturer part number, manufacturer, description and specifications, a marketplace-ready listing title, UPC, MSRP, used market price, and a confidence rate.

03

Needs review

Entries land in a review queue. A person inspects each one and either approves it or overrides the data on the fly — a judgement call on a screen, not another trip to the pallet. The confidence rate tells them where to look first.

04

One button, whole truckload

Once the load is imaged and approved, a single click checks every part number against the database. Anything new is auto-created as a fully loaded product record, attributes and specifications already populated, and the purchase order is generated.

05

Received into stock

The PO is received against the barcode applied back at capture. The unit lands in stock priced and listable, with no re-handling and nothing keyed by hand.

One physical touch, start to stock. The old process read packaging, cross-referenced part numbers, researched manufacturer data and hand-keyed the record — several separate touches per unit, each one a chance to introduce an error. Now the product is handled once. Everything after that, review included, happens on a screen against a record that is already populated.

What that changed

Four compounding gains, not one.

  • Single touch. Product is handled once instead of at every step of identification, data entry and receiving. Review moved from the pallet to the screen.
  • Speed. Identification dropped from minutes of research per unit to well under a minute, and a truckload from 3–4 weeks to about one.
  • Data accuracy. Attributes come from the AI response straight into the record, so transcription errors and half-filled fields largely disappear.
  • Throughput. The same crew clears far more units per shift, because the bottleneck moved from human research to image capture.

Why it compounds

Better records sell faster.

Richer product data is not a back-office nicety in this business. A complete record — correct part number, real specifications, a proper listing title, a defensible price — produces a stronger marketplace listing, and stronger listings get more impressions and convert better.

So the labor savings and the revenue side pull in the same direction: the faster and more accurately product is identified, the sooner it is listed and the better it performs once it is.

The confidence rate is what makes the review queue efficient rather than ceremonial. It tells a reviewer which of the thousands of units on a truck deserve a hard look, instead of spot-checking blind — and a low-confidence entry can be corrected in place before anything is created downstream.

Key takeaway

Deep operational expertise + practical AI.

This is what becomes possible when AI is treated as a solution to a real operating problem rather than as a technology project. The entire process — late-night test to live production — took less than two weeks, without an outside AI consulting team or a months-long implementation.

The ingredients were operational experience, a clear problem and the willingness to test an idea.
