Inventory accuracy case study

From manual cycle counts to real-time reconciliation.

A barcode-driven workflow that compares physical inventory to the ERP on every scan — and reconciles the difference automatically, with no separate cleanup step.

The challenge

The hard part isn’t counting. It’s reconciling.

Cycle counting sounds simple: count what’s physically in a bin and compare it with what the ERP says should be there. The difficult part is resolving the discrepancies — deciding where missing inventory belongs in the system, whether it should remain sellable, who updates its status, and how to correct items that are physically in one bin but expected in another.

Under the existing process, much of that reconciliation was manual and cumbersome. We wanted the physical count itself to drive the reconciliation, instead of a person reconciling it after the fact.

“The employee captures reality. The system reconciles itself to reality.”

The solution

A barcode scan replaces the list.

We built a barcode-driven cycle-counting workflow around each individual unit. Instead of comparing lists by hand, an employee goes to a bin and scans every unit physically present — the system handles the comparison and the reconciliation.

If a unit was expected in the bin but wasn’t scanned, the system marks it missing and moves it to a controlled non-sellable holding status, so it stops being promised to another customer while its location is unknown.

A fully reconciled bin

  • Inventory expected and present — confirmed
  • Inventory expected but missing — held, non-sellable
  • Inventory present but expected elsewhere — location corrected
  • Every exception — logged for follow-up
  • Bin status — fully reconciled at scan-complete
Scan Bin → Compare to ERP → Identify Exceptions → Reconcile Inventory. The opposite problem is handled in the same pass: if a unit is physically in the bin even though the ERP believes it belongs somewhere else, the scan catches the mismatch and updates the location automatically.

Connected, not separate

Tied directly to picking, not a standalone system.

The cycle-counting workflow complements the digital picking process. If a picker can’t find the FIFO-recommended unit in its expected bin, they use an approved override and select another. The missing unit is automatically placed in non-sellable holding — and cycle counting is what later finds and reconciles it.

If that unit turns up in another bin during a count, its location is corrected and it’s returned to an appropriate sellable status — closing the loop between picking exceptions and inventory accuracy.

“Don’t make the employee reconcile the system to reality. Let the employee capture reality, and make the system reconcile itself.”

How it works

Five steps, one scan at a time.

01

Scan the bin

An employee scans every unit physically present. No manual list to work from.

02

System compares

The scan is checked against what the ERP expects to be in that bin.

03

Missing inventory held

Anything expected but not scanned is marked missing and moved to non-sellable holding.

04

Mislocated inventory corrected

Anything scanned but expected elsewhere has its ERP location updated automatically.

05

Bin reconciled

Once scanning is done, the bin is fully reconciled — no follow-up cleanup step.
