AI terms, plain English — trust & safety

Hallucination

When an AI model confidently states something false or made-up, because it’s predicting plausible text, not verifying facts.

Model Drift

When an AI model’s accuracy degrades over time because real-world data has changed since it was built or last checked.

Guardrails

Rules built into an AI system that keep it from taking actions outside an approved scope.

Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

A workflow where a person reviews or approves an AI’s output before it’s acted on, rather than the AI running fully unsupervised.

Grounding

Connecting an AI’s answers to a verified, real source of truth so it can’t drift into invented information.

Confidence Score

A number the AI attaches to its own answer showing how sure it is — low scores flag results for human review.

Human Oversight / Review Queue

A checkpoint where flagged AI outputs wait for a person’s approval before proceeding.

Explainability (XAI)

The degree to which a human can understand why an AI system produced a specific output or decision.

Algorithmic Bias

When an AI system produces skewed or unfair results because of patterns in the data it was trained on.

Alignment

Making sure an AI system’s actions and outputs match what the business actually wants and values, not just what’s technically possible.

AI terms, plain English — how the tech works

Agent Harness

The software layer that runs an AI agent — controls which tools it can use, what data it can touch, and when it stops.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

An AI technique where the model looks up real data before answering, instead of relying only on what it was trained on.

Agentic AI

AI that can take multi-step actions toward a goal on its own — not just answer a question, but complete a task.

Fine-Tuning

Additional training on a general AI model using a company’s own data, so it performs better on that company’s specific task.

Prompt Engineering

The practice of carefully wording instructions given to an AI model to get reliable, accurate output.

Orchestration

Coordinating multiple AI steps or tools in the right order to complete a full workflow, not just one task.

Tool Use / Function Calling

When an AI model reaches outside its own training data mid-task — calling a live database, web search, or API — and uses the real result to finish or check its answer.

Large Language Model (LLM)

The type of AI model trained on huge amounts of text and data to understand and generate language.

Multimodal AI

AI that can process more than one type of input at once — for example, a photo and text together, not just text alone.

Embeddings

A way of converting text, images, or data into numbers that capture their meaning, so an AI can compare and match similar items.

Vector Database

A specialized database that stores embeddings, allowing fast similarity search.

Context Window

The amount of information an AI model can “see” and consider at once when generating a response.

Inference

One run of an AI model — putting information in and getting a result out.

Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

A method where an AI works through a problem in visible, step-by-step reasoning before giving a final answer.

Zero-Shot / Few-Shot Learning

An AI’s ability to handle a new task with little or no prior specific training.

Edge Case

An unusual or rare situation that falls outside an AI system’s normal, well-tested scenarios.

Synthetic Data

Artificially generated data used to train or test an AI system when real data is limited or sensitive.

Sandbox / Sandboxing

A safe, isolated environment where an AI system can be tested without affecting real, live business operations.

Latency

The delay between asking an AI system something and getting a response — lower latency means faster results.

AI terms, plain English — buying decision

Automation Workflow

A defined sequence of steps where an AI (or AI plus a human) completes a business process end-to-end, replacing manual steps.

Benchmark

A standardized test used to compare AI systems’ performance against each other or against a fixed standard.

ROI (AI-specific framing)

Return on investment calculated specifically for an AI project — labor hours saved, error reduction, speed gained, translated into dollars.
